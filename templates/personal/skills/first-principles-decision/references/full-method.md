# Full consequential-decision method

Use this method only when stakes, irreversibility, uncertainty, or downstream effects justify the added depth.

## Frame

- State the decision, owner, decision deadline, and outcome being optimized.
- Ask whether the requirement needs to exist. Remove inherited, performative, or solution-shaped requirements that do not serve the outcome.
- Classify remaining constraints as physical, legal or ethical, contractual, resource-bound, or chosen. Chosen constraints may be changed.

## Ground

Separate the basis of the decision into:

- **Facts:** current observations supported by appropriate evidence.
- **Testimony:** memories, reports, or stakeholder statements that may be useful but require corroboration when consequential.
- **Assumptions:** beliefs carrying the recommendation.
- **Unknowns:** missing information that might change the choice.

Do not research every unknown. Investigate only those with enough decision value to justify the delay or cost.

## Generate real alternatives

Always consider:

- The status quo or doing nothing now.
- The user's proposed path.
- The strongest materially different alternative.
- A smaller reversible experiment when it can answer the load-bearing uncertainty.

Do not pad the list with dominated variations merely to appear balanced.

## Evaluate

Compare alternatives against the outcome and real constraints. Account for:

- Reversibility and option value.
- Cost of delay and opportunity cost.
- First- and second-order effects.
- Operational burden after launch, not only build cost.
- Incentives, dependencies, and likely failure behavior.
- Whether the decision improves learning even if the immediate outcome fails.

Run a short pre-mortem on the leading option. Name the strongest disconfirming evidence and what observation would cause a reversal.

## Decide

Return:

- The recommendation and confidence.
- Why it wins over the strongest rejected alternative.
- The smallest evidence-producing next action.
- A kill criterion or stopping condition when execution can drift.
- A review trigger based on evidence or time, not vague future reconsideration.

When evidence cannot distinguish the options, prefer the path that is reversible, preserves option value, and produces the most decision-relevant learning per unit of cost.
