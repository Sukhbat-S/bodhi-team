# Source strategy

Choose sources by the claim being tested, not by generic prestige.

## Evidence order

1. Direct observation, current code or configuration, original data, or emitted runtime evidence.
2. Current official documentation, law, regulation, standard, filing, dataset, or original research.
3. Independent high-quality analysis that exposes method and sources.
4. First-party statements, expert testimony, and contemporaneous reporting, clearly labeled with incentives and access limits.
5. BODHI memories or private-library material as leads and context, never sole authority for consequential or time-sensitive claims.

Search snippets, aggregators, unsourced summaries, and AI-generated pages are discovery aids, not evidence.

## Match source to claim

- **Product behavior:** current official docs plus emitted behavior when available.
- **Code behavior:** current source, tests, configuration, and runtime signal.
- **Scientific claim:** original paper, methods, data, replications, and current consensus or dispute.
- **Market claim:** original datasets, filings, transaction or usage evidence, with definitions and timeframe.
- **Legal or regulatory claim:** current primary legal text and authoritative regulator or court material; separate information from legal advice.
- **Medical or safety claim:** current authoritative guidance and original evidence; disclose uncertainty and avoid individualized diagnosis.
- **Person or company claim:** direct record plus independent corroboration; distinguish self-report from verified fact.

## Freshness and incentives

Record the event date separately from publication or update date. For volatile claims, verify that the source still describes the current state. Identify who benefits if the claim is believed, what data they can see, and what they cannot observe.

Two sources are not independent when they repeat the same underlying report, dataset, press release, or anonymous claim.
