# Deep dossier method

Use for decisions, durable reports, contested questions, or research spanning several claim families.

## Research contract

Define:

- Question, audience, decision, and deadline.
- Scope and explicit exclusions.
- Required freshness and acceptable uncertainty.
- Claims that would reverse the current hypothesis.
- Stopping condition.

## Claim ledger

For each load-bearing claim record:

- Claim and status: supported, contested, unsupported, or unknown.
- Evidence source and direct location.
- Source class, event date, publication date, and access date.
- What the source actually establishes.
- Limitation, incentive, population, definition, or timeframe.
- Contradictory evidence and resolution status.
- Confidence: high, medium, or low, with a reason.

Do not convert source count into confidence. Confidence depends on source fit, independence, method, directness, freshness, and contradiction survival.

## Adversarial pass

Before synthesis, attack the dossier:

- Find the strongest evidence against the leading conclusion.
- Trace repeated claims back to the originating source.
- Recalculate or sanity-check load-bearing numbers.
- Look for denominator, selection, survivorship, timeframe, and definition changes.
- Discount claims made by parties selling the conclusion.
- Mark unanswered claims instead of bridging them with plausible prose.

## Durable output

When a report was requested, make it independently reviewable:

- Answer-first executive summary.
- Claim ledger or compact evidence table.
- Contradictions and unresolved questions.
- Decision implications without pretending research grants execution authority.
- Inline citations or precise local file references.
- Research date and a re-verification trigger for volatile claims.
